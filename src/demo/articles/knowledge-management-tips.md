# Knowledge Management Tips

A knowledge base is only as good as what you put into it — and how you use it. Here are practical strategies for getting the most out of browzy (or any personal knowledge system).

## Diversify Your Sources

The best insights come from **cross-pollination**. Don't just add articles from one domain:

- Mix academic papers with blog posts
- Combine technical documentation with opinion pieces
- Add your own notes alongside external sources
- Include images, diagrams, and visual references

When browzy synthesizes across diverse sources, it surfaces connections you might have missed.

## Ask Questions Regularly

Your knowledge base isn't a write-only archive. **Querying is how you learn.**

- Ask questions about topics you're studying — the answers will reveal what your wiki knows and what's missing
- Rephrase questions in different ways to explore different angles
- Use follow-up questions to go deeper on specific subtopics
- Ask "how does X relate to Y?" to discover cross-source connections

## Let Gaps Guide You

When browzy says "coverage is thin" or suggests related topics, that's valuable signal. It means:

- Your wiki has the edges of a topic but not the core
- There are related concepts that could strengthen your understanding
- A specific source type (paper, tutorial, overview) might fill the gap

Use gaps as a **reading list generator**. The system is telling you what to learn next.

## Build Incrementally

You don't need to add 50 sources on day one. The most effective approach:

1. **Start small** — add 2-3 sources on a topic you care about right now
2. **Ask questions** — see what the wiki can and can't answer
3. **Fill gaps** — add sources that address weaknesses
4. **Repeat** — your wiki grows organically around your actual interests

## Maintain Quality

Periodically run `/health` to check your wiki's quality. It flags:

- Articles with missing cross-references
- Orphaned content that isn't linked to anything
- Stale sources that might need refreshing

A healthy wiki is a connected wiki. See [[how-browzy-works]] for how the compilation process creates these connections, or [[getting-started]] for a quick refresher on the basics.
