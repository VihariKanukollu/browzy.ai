# What is browzy?

browzy is an LLM-powered personal knowledge base engine. It takes the things you read, watch, and save — URLs, PDFs, images, text files — and compiles them into an interconnected wiki that lives on your machine.

## The Core Loop

The workflow is simple:

1. **Add sources** — paste a URL, drag in a PDF, drop an image. browzy ingests the content and extracts the knowledge.
2. **Compile** — the LLM synthesizes your sources into well-structured markdown articles with cross-references, summaries, and tags.
3. **Ask questions** — type a question in natural language. browzy searches your wiki, gathers context, and gives you an answer grounded in *your* knowledge.
4. **Grow** — every source you add and every question you ask makes your knowledge base smarter. Articles get updated, gaps get filled, connections emerge.

## Your Knowledge, Compiled

Unlike chat-based tools where knowledge disappears after a conversation, browzy **persists everything**. Your wiki is a collection of markdown files you can open in any editor, sync with git, or browse in Obsidian (see [[browzy-vs-alternatives]] for more).

The more you use it, the more valuable it becomes. Cross-source synthesis means browzy can connect ideas from an article you read last month with a PDF you added today.

## What Makes It Different

- **Local-first** — your data stays on your machine, in plain markdown
- **Knowledge compounds** — every source enriches the whole wiki
- **LLM-powered search** — not just keyword matching, but understanding
- **Obsidian-compatible** — your wiki works in any markdown tool
