# browzy vs. Alternatives

There are many ways to manage knowledge. Here's how browzy compares to the tools you might already use.

## vs. ChatGPT / Claude Chat

Chat interfaces are great for one-off questions, but knowledge **evaporates** after each conversation. You can't build on yesterday's research.

browzy is different: every source you add and every question you ask enriches a persistent wiki. Ask the same question next month and the answer draws from everything you've accumulated. Knowledge compounds instead of resetting.

## vs. Obsidian / Logseq

These are excellent note-taking tools, and browzy is actually **compatible** with them — your wiki is plain markdown with `[[wikilinks]]` that Obsidian understands natively.

The difference is **automation**. With Obsidian, you write and link notes manually. With browzy, the LLM does the heavy lifting: extracting key ideas, creating cross-references, synthesizing multiple sources into coherent articles. You add sources; browzy writes the wiki.

Think of browzy as the **research engine** that feeds your Obsidian vault.

## vs. Notion / Evernote

These tools lock your data in proprietary formats and cloud services. browzy stores everything as local markdown files you can:

- Open in any text editor
- Version with git
- Sync however you choose
- Export without vendor lock-in

Your data is yours. No subscription, no lock-in, no cloud dependency.

## vs. RAG Systems

Enterprise RAG tools retrieve chunks of documents. browzy goes further — it **compiles** sources into a structured wiki with articles, cross-references, and concept maps. The result is more coherent than chunk-based retrieval because the LLM has already done the synthesis work.

## The Bottom Line

browzy fills a specific niche: **automated personal knowledge compilation**. It's for people who consume a lot of information and want it organized, searchable, and growing — without spending hours taking notes. See [[what-is-browzy]] for more, or [[getting-started]] to try it yourself.
